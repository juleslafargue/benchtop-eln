Features

Reactions, spectra and inventory in one record

Benchtop understands structures, amounts and spectra, so the record does the arithmetic and the bookkeeping while you do the chemistry.

A stoichiometry table that calculates as you type

Draw the reaction in the built-in structure editor and every reagent appears as a row. Type a mass, volume, mmol or equivalents in any row; the rest follows from the limiting reagent, with purity, density and molarity accounted for.

  • Temperature, time, atmosphere and target concentration as real fields
  • Theoretical mass, % yield and green metrics (atom economy, E-factor, PMI)
  • Hazards for every chemical from PubChem, with warnings for dangerous combinations
  • Repeat at another scale makes the 10 g run from the 1 g one

NMR processing and analysis in the browser

Upload a zipped Bruker experiment folder; you don't need to process it in TopSpin first. Benchtop runs the Fourier transform, phases the spectrum, finds the signals and scales the integrals to your product's formula.

  • Predicted ¹H and ¹³C shifts from NMRShiftDB2, matched atom by atom
  • Residual solvents recognised and reported as mol% and wt%
  • JCAMP-DX, Bruker raw FIDs and processed spectra, CSV
  • An HRMS helper that writes the calcd for …; found … line

A Getting started guide

Eleven short parts on the everyday steps, from making your own notebook to signing and witnessing, with links into the app. It's in the menu and the Ctrl K (⌘K) box, and a newcomer's dashboard points to it. Empty pages say what goes there.

Keyboard and screen readers

Skip to content is the first Tab stop, and closing a dialog puts the focus back where it was. Every text box and amount cell has a name a screen reader can read out ("Mass (mg), benzyl azide"), and the experiment page has headings to jump between.

Five colour themes, light and dark

Benchtop, Lab notebook, Fume hood, Litmus and High contrast, each in light and dark. Text meets the WCAG AA contrast minimum in all of them, checked with axe-core. Your choice follows you between computers.

Signing and audit trail

Signed records are locked and verifiable

Benchtop follows the author-and-witness routine of a paper notebook, and adds the checks a computer can do.

  1. Draft

    Write freely. Every save of every section is kept as a version you can look back at.

  2. Signed

    You sign with your password and the record locks. The signature stores a SHA-256 fingerprint of everything in it, files included.

    sha256 4fd28d3b74dd44673fd89bb0…

  3. Witnessed

    A colleague you choose confirms it, or returns it with a reason. Records can be voided, never deleted.

Trusted timestamps

Each signature is certified by an independent RFC 3161 timestamp authority (DigiCert by default), the standard used for patent evidence. Anyone can check the proof with openssl, without trusting Benchtop.

An audit trail that can't be quietly edited

Every change, sign-in and failed signature attempt goes into a log where each entry carries the fingerprint of the one before. Verify finds anything altered, removed or reordered. Admins read it in plain sentences, with each event as stored underneath.

Backups that are tested

Daily backups, a second copy on another drive, and once a week Benchtop restores the newest one in a scratch folder to prove it works. ./benchtop check verifies everything on demand.

A daily check of the data in use

Once a day Benchtop checks the database, the audit trail, every signature, the trusted timestamps and every attached file. A failing disk, or a file changed outside Benchtop, shows up the next day; a damaged file can be put back from the backups with one click.

Warnings before the disk is full

Admins are warned on the dashboard and by email when the disk with the data or the backups has less than 5 GB free. The last 1 GB is kept for saving what people type: an upload or import that would use it is refused, with a plain message.

Unsaved typing kept in your browser

If Benchtop stops, the Wi-Fi goes or your sign-in runs out, what you typed stays in your browser until it's saved. Close the tab, or let the browser crash, and the section offers to put it back next time.

A reminder before signing

The sign dialog lists anything still empty: a section, the isolated mass, hazards not recorded. It's only a reminder: it never stops you signing or changes what the signature covers.

Old notebooks archived, not deleted

An archived notebook leaves the sidebar and takes no new experiments; its records stay readable, searchable and signed, and Unarchive brings it back. Only a notebook with no experiments in it can be deleted.

Integrations

Instruments, university sign-in, other notebooks and Zenodo

Instrument folders

Point Benchtop at the folders your NMR, LC-MS and HPLC save to. A file named with an experiment number or a sample code (JL-013) attaches itself to the right draft. Anything uncertain waits in an Inbox; Benchtop never guesses.

Moving records in and out

Import from and export to the .eln format that other lab notebooks share. Each record travels with its report, every file with its fingerprint, structures as MOL/RXN and its signature history. Each export states its licence: all rights reserved, or CC BY or CC0 when it's published on Zenodo.

Sign in with your university account

Single sign-on with Microsoft 365, Google Workspace or any OpenID Connect provider, optionally limited to your email domain. Or plain accounts with admin, scientist and viewer roles.

Microsoft 365Google WorkspaceOpenID Connect

Groups and sharing

Research groups with their leads. Each notebook is open to everyone, to one group, or private; share a single experiment to view or edit. People only ever see records they may open.

Comments and your phone

Threads on any section with @mentions, even on signed records. Press P for a QR code that opens the experiment on your phone, to photograph a TLC plate or run a timer one-handed.

Your own additions

Plugin files add section types such as a glovebox log or a hydrogenation checklist, with no code. API tokens and webhooks for scripts, Slack or Teams, and a Markdown export for Obsidian.

Publishing on Zenodo, with a DOI

Publish a signed record on Zenodo as an open dataset, with a DOI that a thesis, a paper's supporting information or a funder's data rules can cite. What goes public is the record's PDF and its .eln export, with every attached file and its SHA-256.

ZenodoORCID iDsCC BY 4.0CC0 1.0
  • A preview first of everything that will be public: the files, title, authors, keywords, licence and the DOIs it cites
  • Two steps: a draft on Zenodo with a reserved DOI, which you can look at and discard, then publishing, which can't be undone
  • Each person publishes from their own Zenodo account, and can try it on Zenodo's sandbox first
  • The signed record itself never changes, and verifies against its signatures exactly as before

Install

Install Benchtop

Benchtop runs on one computer and everyone else uses it in their browser. Choose the computer it will run on.

  1. Install Python 3.11 or newer from python.org, if you don't have it.

  2. Download benchtop-eln-vX.Y.Z.zip from the Releases page and unzip it.

  3. Double-click Benchtop.command in the folder. The first start sets everything up, then opens your browser, where you create your administrator account.

  4. If macOS says it couldn't verify the file, click Done, then System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway.

Try it with realistic data first

A demo lab with three months of a fictional group's work, kept apart from your own notebook:

./benchtop demo

Updating later is one command, ./benchtop update, which backs up first; ./benchtop rollback undoes it.

  1. Check you have Python 3.11 or newer (python3 --version; Ubuntu 24.04 has 3.12), then install what the launcher uses:

    sudo apt update && sudo apt install -y python3-venv curl unzip

  2. Download benchtop-eln-vX.Y.Z.zip from the Releases page, then unzip it and start Benchtop:

    cd ~ && unzip ~/Downloads/benchtop-eln-v*.zip
cd ~/benchtop && ./benchtop

  3. Open http://localhost:8000 if your browser doesn't open by itself, and create your administrator account.

Same launcher as on a Mac

Everything else works the same: ./benchtop demo, update, rollback, backups, and ./benchtop lab to share it with labmates on your network.

These steps are for Ubuntu and Debian; other distributions need the same three packages.

  1. Labmates on Windows don't install anything. When Benchtop runs on a Mac or a lab server, they open it in Edge or Chrome like any website.

  2. To run your own copy, install WSL, Microsoft's free Linux inside Windows. In PowerShell as administrator:

    wsl --install

    Restart, open Ubuntu from the Start menu, and follow the Linux steps there.

  3. Unzip and run Benchtop inside Ubuntu, not from a Windows folder, then open http://localhost:8000 in Edge or Chrome.

Good to know

Installing WSL needs an administrator, which a university-managed PC may not give you. The README's On Windows section has every step.

A double-click starter for Windows itself is on the roadmap.

  1. Same network: run ./benchtop lab on a Mac or Linux computer that stays on. Labmates open the address it prints.

  2. From anywhere: the same computer with Tailscale (see Working from home below), or a small Linux server with HTTPS, single sign-on and off-site backups using the included Docker setup.

    docker compose -f deploy/docker-compose.server.yml --env-file deploy/.env up -d --build

  3. Give each person their own account and put them in a group. Updates are one command: deploy/update-server.sh.

Working from home

Labmates can use the lab's Benchtop from home through Tailscale, a free app that joins their laptop or phone to a private network with the lab computer. Benchtop stays off the public internet: only people the lab invites can reach it, and they still sign in with their own Benchtop account.

The full guide, for labmates and for the admin who sets it up: Using Benchtop from home.

  1. The admin sets up Tailscale on the lab computer once (about 20 minutes) and invites each labmate.

  2. Each labmate installs Tailscale, accepts the invite and turns it on (about 5 minutes).

  3. They open the lab's Benchtop address, like https://lab-mac.tail1234.ts.net, and sign in as usual. Signing still asks for their password.
